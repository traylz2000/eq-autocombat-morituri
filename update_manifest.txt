note=## 2026-10-31
note=
note=- **The puller kills everything at camp before the next pull when you're solo.** A mob standing next to you could be left hitting you while the puller ran out for another. The step that picks up mobs already on your extended target skips any it has marked unreachable (after a few "You cannot see your target" retries, or 15 s without reaching it), any outside the height filter and any past the chase range. Now in Camp and Lure, before going out, MoriAC checks your extended target. A mob within 40 units is targeted and fought, even one marked unreachable. A mob further out holds the next pull for up to 20 s in case it walks in. **Clear camp first** on the **Pulling** card sets when this applies: **Solo** (the default: only when you're not in a group, so nobody else is there to tank), **Always**, or **Off**. Mobs on your ignore list don't count.
note=
note=- **The puller skips a mob it can't hurt.** A mob still at 100% HP after 10 s of your attacks (immune to your hits, or hit from a spot that never lands) is now skipped for 10 minutes, and the puller moves on. Only time spent attacking it from reach counts, not the run home with it chasing you. Only that one mob is skipped, not every mob with its name, so it isn't added to your ignore list. Set the time with **Skip if unhurt after** on the **Pulling** card (0 = never). This applies to Hunt, Camp and Lure.
note=
note=- **Tradeskills no longer stops with "Combat started" at a quiet forge (`lua/tac/tradeskill.lua` 1.0.7).** It used MoriAC's full combat check. That counts the whole group's haters (a groupmate fighting across the zone), auto-attack left on, the game's combat state and an engaged target, so a player at a forge could get "Made 0 ... - Combat Started" on the first click. Now a run stops only when a live NPC on your own extended target is within 150 units, and the message names it, e.g. "combat started: a minotaur is on your XTarget, 40 away". **Make** also won't start while such a mob is there, and says why in chat and in the Tradeskills log.
note=
note=- **Tradeskills no longer says "Combine did not take" after a good combine (`lua/tac/tradeskill.lua` 1.0.6).** The combines were working, but successes weren't recognised. The server sends the success line with the recipe name filled in, so the line on your client doesn't end in the bare "...something new!" that MoriAC looked for. Every success was counted as a click that didn't take, failures reset that count, and a few successes in a row ended the run early. Now any line starting "You have fashioned the items together" is a success (a groupmate's "X has successfully fashioned Y!" doesn't count). As a backup, the product turning up on your cursor or in your bags also counts, so a reworded line can't stall a run again.
note=
note=- **Tradeskills combines the way your own social macro does (`lua/tac/tradeskill.lua` 1.0.5).** It now presses Combine with `/notify TradeskillWnd CombineButton leftmouseup`, the command a working social uses. The Window `LeftMouseUp()` call it used before didn't press this button. Then it sends `/autoinventory` 0.2 s, 0.5 s and 0.8 s after the click: the social's two plus a third that the in-game socials don't have room for. After that it reads the result line, waits until Combine can be clicked again plus your **Wait between combines** setting, and clicks again. A click that gets no answer is tried again, and the slider is no longer raised by itself. It still stops when you're out of materials, at the count, in combat or when your bags are full.
note=
note=- **Tradeskills waits out the greyed-out Combine button (`lua/tac/tradeskill.lua` 1.0.4).** Combine greys out for about 2.5-3 s after each combine while the recipe refreshes. MoriAC could click it before it was ready. The click did nothing, it waited 8 s for an answer, and three of those ended the run with "No answer from the server for that combine". Now it waits (up to 8 s) until Combine is clickable and still clickable 0.3 s later. A click that gets no answer within 3 s is tried again instead of counting toward stopping. Each one also adds 1 s to the new **Wait between combines** setting (up to 6 s), which covers a client that never shows the button as grey. You can set it yourself; 0 means click as soon as the button is ready. A run only stops after 6 dead clicks in a row. With **Debug info** on (right-click the window), the log shows how long each combine waited for the button and for the answer.
note=
note=- **Tradeskills runs at full speed (`lua/tac/tradeskill.lua` 1.0.3).** The real cause of the slow combines: every wait in a run (for the result, for the cursor to empty, for the row to select) was only checked once per pass of MoriAC's main loop. Each pass sleeps 150 ms, and the plugin only got a turn every 0.1 s, so each step cost 150-250 ms whatever the timers said. During a run the main loop now does short 10 ms passes and the plugin gets a turn every pass. This goes back to normal a second after the run ends. The fixed waits are also shorter: 0.05 s between combines, and 0.2 s to make sure nothing else is queued on the cursor. Plugins can ask for short passes through the new `core.fastLoop(sec)`.
note=
note=- **Tradeskills puts results away right after Combine (`lua/tac/tradeskill.lua` 1.0.2).** There was too long a pause between combines. The product waited on the cursor until the next pass of the loop, and the check that nothing else is queued on the cursor waited 0.4 s even when nothing had been there, twice per combine. Now an item goes into your bags the moment it reaches the cursor, even if that's before the result line. The queue check only runs after something was put away, and it's 0.25 s.
note=
note=- **Tradeskills clears the cursor before every combine (`lua/tac/tradeskill.lua` 1.0.1).** It pressed Combine again while results were still coming onto the cursor. A combine can put several items there (the product plus salvage, or several results), and the next one shows up a moment after the one before goes into your bags. The plugin did one auto-inventory, saw an empty cursor for an instant and combined again, so items piled up and the run fell apart. Now it keeps putting items away until the cursor has stayed empty for 0.4 s, and waits up to 1.5 s for the product to arrive after a success. It only stops for full bags when one item won't go into your bags for 3 s.
note=
note=- **New Tradeskills plugin (`lua/tac/tradeskill.lua`): make a recipe until you run out of materials.** Open a tradeskill container (forge, oven, loom, kit...) so the game tradeskill window shows, then open **Tradeskills** (`/ac ts`). It lists the recipes in the game window (your favourites, or whatever you search for there). Pick one, set **How many** (0 = until out of materials) and press **Make**. MoriAC selects the recipe in the game window and presses **Combine** over and over. The server takes the ingredients from your bags. Whatever lands on your cursor (the product, or salvage from a failed combine) goes into your bags before the next combine. It stops when the server says you're out of materials (and lists what's missing), or when you reach the count, combat starts, the window closes or your bags are full. It also stops when the recipe needs a different container, a skill you don't have or a recipe you haven't learned. **Stop once the recipe is trivial** ends the run when you can no longer skill up from it. Each run ends with a summary: made, failed, skill-ups. Pause MoriAC while crafting so it doesn't go pulling.
note=
note=- **The puller goes for the mob that's closest to walk to.** It used to take the first mob that passed its filters, which wasn't always the nearest. A mob on your floor 95 units away beat one on a slope 20 units away, because mobs more than 15 units above or below you were only looked at when nothing was on your floor. A mob behind a wall could beat one in the open that was a little further away. Now every mob that qualifies is compared by its walking distance (the nav path). A mob on another floor still loses unless it's clearly closer. In Camp and Lure, when you're more than 30 units from camp (a fight that drifted, a Lure that went out to the mob), mobs are ranked by how close they are to the camp. Camp and Lure also get the **Same-floor height** slider that Hunt already had, under **Camp & pull area**. Raise it on hilly ground. This applies to Hunt, Camp and Lure.
